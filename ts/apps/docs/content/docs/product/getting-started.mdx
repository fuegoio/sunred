Sunred runs in the browser, so there's nothing to install. Sign in, subscribe to a feed, and you'll be reading your first article in a couple of minutes.

## Sign in

Open your Sunred instance and sign in with your AT Proto handle — the same account you use on Bluesky or any other AT Proto app. There's no separate Sunred password to create or remember.

If your instance has a default PDS configured, you'll see a **Continue with snrd.social** button (or similar) that takes you straight through. To sign in with an existing handle instead, click **Login with a different handle** and enter it — `alice.bsky.social`, for example.

Sunred redirects you to your PDS to approve the login, then brings you back signed in. If you're curious what's happening behind the scenes, see [Sign in and sync with AT Proto](/at-proto).

## Subscribe to your first feed

Click the **+** next to **Feeds** in the sidebar and paste a website or feed URL — `https://blog.golang.org`, for example. A regular website URL is fine; Sunred discovers the feed automatically.

Before you commit, Sunred shows a preview with the feed's title, description, and most recent articles. If it looks right, click **Subscribe to this feed**.

The feed's title and site link fill in after the first refresh. New articles start appearing in your timeline as soon as Sunred fetches them.

## Read an entry

Your timeline collects entries from every feed, newest first, with a dot marking the unread ones. Click a title to open the original article in a new tab — Sunred marks it read as you open it. To read inside Sunred instead, click anywhere else on the card and the in-app reader opens.

Three things worth trying in your first session. Click the **star** icon on a card to bookmark an article; starred articles live under **Starred** in the sidebar. Click the **share** icon to pass an article on to people who follow you. Articles the people you follow share show up in your timeline alongside your own subscriptions. And the **search** box at the top of the sidebar searches every entry by title and content.

## Group feeds into folders

Once you have a handful of subscriptions, folders keep the sidebar tidy. Click the **folder** icon next to **Feeds** to create one — "Technology" or "News", say — then move feeds into it from a feed's page using the folder picker.

Folders only change how feeds are grouped in the sidebar. The timeline ignores them.
